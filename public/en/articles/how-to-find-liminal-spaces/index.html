How to Find and Photograph Liminal Spaces

Published: 2026-08-15

Liminal spaces aren't hiding in some exotic, faraway location. Most of the time, all it takes to find one is changing the time of day or the angle you look at a place you already pass through constantly. Here's how to spot them nearby and photograph them well.

You Don't Need a Rare Location

The most important mindset for finding liminal spaces isn't "go somewhere unusual" — it's "look for the other face of somewhere familiar." The station you pass through every day, or the mall down the street, can become a striking liminal space depending on the time or circumstances. Start by simply asking yourself: what would this place look like with absolutely no one in it?

The Best Times to Look

The easiest finds come from moments with the biggest gap from a place's usual energy: a station between the last and first trains, a shop right before opening or right after closing, or a residential or office district early on a weekend morning before anyone's out. Knowing how lively a place usually is makes the emptiness hit that much harder.

Types of Places Worth Checking

Station corridors and platforms, parking garages, underground malls, hospital or hotel hallways, danchi stairwells, and school or public-facility corridors are all structurally prone to becoming liminal spaces. What they share: symmetry, a repeating rhythm, and a vanishing point in the distance. The more of these features a space has, the stronger the photo tends to turn out.

Photography Tips

Stand in the center of the hallway or corridor and frame the shot symmetrically toward the vanishing point at the far end — that composition alone does a lot of the work. Let the ceiling lights and floor reflections show as they naturally are; that's what gives the shot its distinctive even brightness. And try to catch the moment with absolutely no one in frame — a single person instantly turns the photo back into an ordinary everyday scene.

Safety and Etiquette

Never enter private property or restricted areas without permission, and avoid disturbing other visitors or staff at any location that's still operating. For abandoned buildings specifically, structural decay poses a real accident risk, and there are ownership rights to consider — it's best to avoid forcing your way onto the property and instead photograph from the exterior or from public roads.

FAQ

Do I need special equipment to find liminal spaces?

No — a smartphone camera is plenty. A camera with a wide-angle option makes it easier to build a shot with strong depth.

Is a photo ruined if someone accidentally walks into frame?

Not necessarily, but an empty frame emphasizes the "place you pass through" context much more strongly, which is usually what gives a liminal space photo its effect.
