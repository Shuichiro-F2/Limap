Liminal Space vs. the Backrooms: What's the Difference?

Published: 2026-08-15

Yellow wallpaper, humming fluorescent lights, a hallway that never ends — liminal spaces and the Backrooms look so alike that people mix them up constantly. But the two are fundamentally different in origin and nature.

The Short Answer: Real Place vs. Fictional World

The biggest difference, in one line: a liminal space is a real place, while the Backrooms is a fictional world built as a story. One is a real environment you can photograph; the other belongs to the horror and creepypasta genre.

What a Liminal Space Is

A liminal space is the real-world style of a place — a hallway, a parking lot, an empty station — once the people are gone and it's reduced to "a place you pass through." There's no story and no threat baked in; the unease comes purely from a sense of incompleteness. See our full definition article for more.

What the Backrooms Is

The Backrooms is an urban legend that grew out of a single image posted to an anonymous imageboard in 2019: a yellow-carpeted, damp-walled maze of endless rooms lit by humming fluorescent tubes. A fictional premise was added — that you "noclip" out of reality into this space — and the idea spread rapidly through online creative communities.

In 2022, a found-footage-style video by then-16-year-old creator Kane Pixels went viral and turned the concept into something much bigger: a survival-horror world complete with wire-like entities that chase you, expanding the Backrooms into its own ongoing fictional universe.

The Key Difference: Narrative and Threat

A liminal space photo doesn't tell you a story on its own. Nothing is chasing you, nothing is about to happen — the discomfort comes from incompleteness alone. The Backrooms, by contrast, was built as narrative from day one. Simply being there implies a plot: how did you get here, and what happens next? A sense of threat is baked into the premise.

Why They Get Confused

The main reason people mix them up is that they look nearly identical. Fluorescent lights, uniform flooring, an endless hallway — the Backrooms aesthetic was literally built on top of liminal space photography, so the visual overlap is huge, and the two get tagged interchangeably on social media all the time.

FAQ

Is the Backrooms a real place?

No. The Backrooms is a piece of fiction/urban legend that originated in 2019 — it isn't a real location. Liminal spaces, by contrast, are always real places.

Do liminal space photos always feel scary?

Not necessarily. Many people describe nostalgia or a strange sense of familiarity rather than fear — the reaction varies a lot from person to person.
