Are the Backrooms Real? Where the Original Photo Was Taken, and Places in Japan That Feel the Same

Published: 2026-08-26 / Updated: 2026-10-03

"Is there really a place like that somewhere?" The Backrooms — an endless maze of yellow-wallpapered rooms lit only by humming fluorescent tubes — has become a horror-culture phenomenon. Does anything like it actually exist in Japan? This article briefly traces the legend's origins, then rounds up real places with a similar atmosphere, using actual examples recorded on LIMap.

What Are the Backrooms? A Quick Recap of the 4chan Legend

The Backrooms is an urban legend (creepypasta) that spread after a single photo was posted to the anonymous imageboard 4chan in 2019. The premise: an empty, yellow-wallpapered office-like space that stretches on forever like a maze — somewhere you might accidentally "noclip" into from reality. The defining details are almost always the same: the low hum of fluorescent lights, and the smell of damp carpet.

The concept quickly expanded online, with fans adding a whole hierarchy of "Levels" and creatures of their own. A feature film adaptation is also known to be in the works, turning what started as a single forum post into a full-blown piece of internet culture.

The Short Answer: The Endless Rooms Are Fiction, but the Original Photo Is Real

Let's get this out of the way first: the Backrooms as an endless maze of rooms is not a real place. It is a piece of fiction that grew out of a 2019 forum post, and the Levels and creatures were all added later by people online.

The single photo that started it all, however, does have a real location. In 2024 it was traced to a building in Oshkosh, Wisconsin, USA: a room photographed in 2002 while it was being renovated (we cover the full story in "Where Was the Original Backrooms Photo Taken?"). It was not taken in Japan, and the room was put to a different use after the renovation, so you cannot visit the yellow room as it appears in the photo.

That said, plenty of people describe a nagging sense of familiarity when they see Backrooms imagery. Tracing that feeling back leads to real places with a surprisingly similar atmosphere.

What "Liminal Space" Has to Do With That Yellow Room

Part of why the Backrooms resonates with so many people is that it takes the feeling known online as a "liminal space" and pushes it to an extreme. A liminal space is that unsettling feeling when everyone suddenly vanishes from a place that's normally full of people (we go deeper into this in What Is a Liminal Space?).

An empty office lit only by fluorescent tubes, a train platform at 2am, a shopping mall after closing — the Backrooms' yellow room might just be the most distilled, abstracted version of that same feeling.

Real Places With a Similar Atmosphere Do Exist Across Japan

The room itself doesn't exist, but real places with a similar atmosphere are scattered across Japan. LIMap already has several of them recorded.

Take Capsule Inn Osaka, the world's first practical capsule hotel (opened 1979) — a surreal, uniform space where identical pods repeat endlessly down the corridor. Or Higashi-Narita Station, once a terminal stop for Narita Airport and now a quietly frozen-in-time station with a dim, empty concourse. Each one carries that same feeling: nobody's there, but something still lingers.

No. It shows a room in a building in Oshkosh, Wisconsin, USA, photographed while the building was being renovated. The full story is in "Where Was the Original Backrooms Photo Taken?"

Are there places like the Backrooms in Japan?
